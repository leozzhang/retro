# Rosters: FBS teams and how their players are generated

Every FBS team in 2026 (138) has a researched **profile** (`src/data/fbs/profiles.ts`). The roster generator (`src/data/rosterGen.ts`) turns each profile into an 85-man roster, seeded by the team id, so the same team always gets the same players. Team overalls are **not** stored anywhere: `teamRatings` computes them from whoever is healthy and starting (`src/data/depth.ts`). For what each rating does on the field, see [RATINGS.md](RATINGS.md).

## The research
- **Date:** 2026-10-24, mid-season. Seven research agents covered the conferences in parallel, from team-season pages (Wikipedia "2026 X football team"), conference standings, AP rankings, preseason all-conference teams and award watch lists, and school roster pages. Sources for every team are in the tables below.
- **Raw data:** `docs/research/profiles_*.json`. To change a team, edit its JSON and run `python scripts/build_profiles.py > tables.md` from the repo root. It validates everything (positions, archetypes, colors, home state, unique ids and abbreviations), rewrites `profiles.ts`, and prints the tables in this file.
- **What a profile holds:**
  - conference, colors, **tier** (1 national power to 5 bottom FBS or new to FBS) and **prestige** (tradition, 1-100)
  - the 2026 record so far
  - **targets:** the overall of a typical starter on offense, defense and special teams, on a 55-95 scale (90+ = title contender unit, 62-67 = weak G5). Based on record, AP rank, recruiting talent and returning production, blended with tradition.
  - **offense:** the starting QB's style (`pocket`, `dual`, `runner`) and the scheme (`air-raid`, `spread`, `pro`, `option`, `power-run`). These only steer which archetypes the team carries; the sim doesn't read them.
  - **units:** position groups clearly above or below the rest of the team (-2..+2)
  - **standouts:** real players on the 2026 roster (486 in all), each with position, class, redshirt, archetype and tier (`elite` All-American, `star` all-conference, `good` quality starter)
- **Membership notes for 2026:**
  - Pac-12 (8 football schools): Boise State, Colorado State, Fresno State, Oregon State, San Diego State, Texas State, Utah State, Washington State.
  - Mountain West (10): adds UTEP, Northern Illinois (football only) and North Dakota State (football only, new to FBS).
  - MAC (13): adds Sacramento State (football only, new to FBS). UMass is a MAC member.
  - Louisiana Tech moved to the Sun Belt. C-USA has 10, with Delaware and Missouri State in their second FBS year.
  - Independents: Notre Dame and UConn.

### Known weak spots in the research
- **Class years and redshirt flags** are often inferred (sources rarely list them), especially for defensive standouts taken from preseason all-conference lists. They may be off by a year.
- **QB situations in flux** (injuries and benchings as of Oct 24): Ohio State (Julian Sayin hurt, Luke Fahey starting), UCLA (Nico Iamaleava hurt; Ty Dieffenbach listed), Oregon (Dante Moore and Dylan Raiola both listed), Michigan (Bryce Underwood benched; Tommy Carr listed too), Tennessee (George MacIntyre replaced Faizon Brandon), Clemson (Tait Reynolds), Syracuse, Texas Tech, Colorado, Arizona State, UCF, Navy, Temple, Washington State, Tulsa and several Sun Belt and C-USA teams. Where two QBs are listed, the better-rated one starts.
- **Thin 2026 information** (standouts lean on preseason lists): Navy, Rice, Temple, UAB, Utah State, Colorado State, Georgia State, Sam Houston, Missouri State, Southern Miss, and much of the MAC.
- **No in-season SP+ or FPI** was found; targets rest on record, AP rank, preseason polls and honors.
- **QB style labels** are judged from rushing numbers where sources don't say.
- **Colors** for Sun Belt and C-USA come from general knowledge, not a source.
- Missouri's Ahmad Hardy (status unknown after a May 2026 shooting) and Boise State's kicker (conflicting sources) were left out on purpose.

## How a roster is generated
1. **Composition (85):** QB 4, HB 5, FB 1, WR 10, TE 4, OT 6, OG 7, C 3, DE 8, DT 7, OLB 6, MLB 4, CB 10, FS 3, SS 3, K 2, P 2.
2. **Unit targets:** the side's target plus 2.5 per `units` step, re-centered so each side still averages its target (the research's strengths and weaknesses are relative to the team).
3. **Standouts** go in first under their real names, at a level set by their tier: `good` at the unit target, `star` at target +3 (at least 76), `elite` at target +7 (at least 84).
4. **Everyone else** gets a class (28% FR, 26% SO, 24% JR, 22% SR; 35-50% redshirted) and a level from it (FR -8, SO -3, JR +1, SR +3, +1.5 for a redshirt year) with a 5-point spread. The group is shifted so the position's starters, standouts included, average the unit target.
5. **Archetypes** come from odds per position, bent by the scheme (option teams carry more running QBs and power backs; air raid more deep and slot receivers; pro and power teams blocking tight ends). With no researched QB, the best generated QB plays the team's QB style.
6. **Height and weight** are drawn per position, correlated with each other, and **nudge ratings**: heavier means stronger and a step slower; taller receivers win more contested balls; taller DBs press better. The sim never reads height or weight.
7. **Ratings** come from the position template at the player's level (see RATINGS.md), plus the archetype, the build nudge and a little noise. The skill ratings are then shifted together until his overall lands on his level.
8. **Calibration:** starters are picked by overall from a noisy pool, which would otherwise beat the levels they were rolled at, so each position's generated starters are shifted to average their aim exactly. Then any standout who fell outside his position's starters is lifted just above the last generated starter, so researched players start.
9. **Potential and stars** come last: potential is overall plus the growth left for his class (FR 10, SO 6, JR 3, SR 1), scaled by a development trait (normal, impact, star, elite). Recruiting stars come from potential.
10. **Hometowns:** a share from the school's state (28% for tier 1 up to 60% for tier 5), 30% from its region, and the rest national, weighted by each state's output of FBS recruits.
11. **Jerseys** (`JERSEY`, `pickJersey` in `rosterGen.ts`), on a stream of their own, so numbers never move a rating: each position draws from weighted bands the way college programs hand them out (QBs 1-19; WRs single digits, teens and 80s; backs 0-9 and 20s-30s; FBs 20s-40s; TEs 80s and 40s; the line 50-79; DTs the 90s and 50s-60s; edge rushers the 90s, 40s-50s and some single digits; backers and DBs 0-39 or 0-59). The best players choose first, and single digits (zero among them) lean toward them (`JERSEY.prestige`). Only when a position's bands are full does a player share a number, and then only with someone on the other side of the ball. Joiners pick the same way when they arrive: signees by stars and asking for their high school number, transfers keeping theirs if it's free, placeholder freshmen last.

**Result:** across the 138 teams, computed team ratings land within about half a point of the research targets on average, and tier-1 programs average well above tier 3, which average well above tier 5 (tests in `logic.test.ts`, `describe('rosters')`).

## Depth chart and injuries
- `buildDepth` lists, for each on-field slot, everyone who can play it, by overall at that slot's position. A player out of position (a safety at nickel, a guard at tackle) rates 4 lower there.
- `starters` fills the slots in a fixed order (QB, RB, X, Z, SL, TE, line, ..., then defense), taking the first healthy player on each list who isn't already starting. So the best receiver is the X, the next the Z, and so on.
- `Athlete.injury = { weeks, type, season? }` takes a player off the field. His backup starts on the next snap, and `teamRatings` drops right away. `Athlete.health` below 100 means he's banged up: he plays with `fitRatings` and the staff chart may rest him. Both come from the season's injury model ([INJURIES.md](INJURIES.md)).
